# What is interactive fiction?

An interactive fiction is a story told in text that you play. The game describes a place and what you notice there;
you answer with what you want to do, in a few words; the game tells you what happens next. You read, you act, the
story moves on, and where it goes depends on you.

> You are standing at the foot of a lighthouse. The door is ajar, and a spiral stair climbs into the dark.
>
> \> **climb the stair**
>
> The steps are worn hollow in the middle. Halfway up, you hear the sea booming against the rocks below…

There is no picture to look at (or very few), no timer and no reflexes to train: these are games for readers. Some
are short stories you finish in a quarter of an hour, others are long adventures full of puzzles that keep you busy
for weeks. There are mysteries, horror, comedies, science fiction, love stories, games for children and games about
nothing more than a quiet afternoon.

## A short history

The first of these games, *Adventure*, was written in the 1970s on university computers: you explored a cave by
typing `go north` or `take lamp`. In the 1980s they became best sellers, sold in boxes full of maps and souvenirs,
under the name of "text adventures". The boxes are long gone, but the games never stopped: since the 1990s,
thousands of independent authors have written new ones, often for yearly competitions, and they give most of them
away for free. Interactive fiction has become a small, lively literature of its own.

The Interactive Fiction Database (IFDB) lists these games, with players' reviews and ratings, and the IF Archive keeps
their files. Inkventure reads its catalogue from the first and downloads its games from the second.

## Two ways to play

**Typing commands.** In most classic games, called *parser* games, you type short commands such as `open the door`
or `ask the keeper about the storm`. The game understands a few hundred words; part of the fun is finding what to do
and how to say it. The next chapter shows how, and Inkventure offers buttons for the most common commands, so you
rarely need the keyboard.

**Choosing.** In *choice-based* games, the story offers a few options at the end of each passage and you tap one.
They read like a gamebook and need no typing at all.

Inkventure plays both: parser games made with Inform and similar tools (in the formats called *Z-machine* and
*Glulx*), and choice-based games made with *ink* and *Twine*. Twine games are marked *Experimental*: they are web
pages of their own, and some were made for a computer screen rather than for e-ink.
