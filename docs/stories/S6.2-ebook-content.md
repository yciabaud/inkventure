## User story

As a curious reader, I want a short, friendly guide to interactive fiction so that I know how to play before opening the app.

## Scope

- Chapters: What is IF · How to play (commands, compass, verbs, saving) · Using Inkventure on your e-reader (Wi-Fi, browser, where saves are kept) · Featured games · Credits.
- EN and FR versions; tone reviewed by the owner.
- Screenshots of the app in grayscale.

## Notes & decisions

- **Chapters** (both editions, same outline): Welcome (the "Open Inkventure" link, what the book holds) · What is
  interactive fiction? (an example exchange, a short history, parser vs. choice-based games, the formats played;
  Twine marked experimental) · How to play (commands, the compass with abbreviations, when you are stuck, choice
  games, saving: autosave, Undo, five slots, Restart; the game's own SAVE/RESTORE are not used; playing with the
  buttons) · Inkventure on your e-reader (opening the browser, Wi-Fi only to open the app and download a game, Home
  and Library, page turns, the reader's menu and Aa, Refresh screen, where games are kept, what clears them, how the
  app frees space) · Games to start with (the cards, unchanged from S6.1) · Credits (games, IFDB, IF Archive,
  interpreters, fonts, MIT licence, the download page). The draft notice is gone.
- **Words:** button and menu names are the app's own strings (`src/i18n/`), so the book and the app agree. The
  French chapter gives French commands (as the app's French buttons send them) and the English ones, since many games
  are in English. E-readers and their browsers stay generic: no brand names (CLAUDE.md).
- **Not claimed:** the reader has no "▸ more" marker (SPEC §3.6 mentions one; the page indicator is described
  instead). Difficulty is still the "Start here" badge (S6.1).
- **Screenshots:** `scripts/ebook/screenshots.ts` (`npm run ebook:screenshots`, after `npm run build`) starts
  `vite preview` on the build, blocks every other host, and captures five screens per locale at 600 × 800 (Home on a
  first launch, a parser game with the object row, the reader's menu, the Aa settings, a choice game) from the sample
  catalogue and the fixture games. Each PNG is turned to 16 gray levels in the page (canvas) and written with the
  QR codes' grayscale encoder: 5–14 KB each, committed in `ebook/<locale>/images/`. The fixture stories are English, so
  the French screenshots show the French UI with an English game; the captions say so. The French Featured shelf of
  the sample has one game. Pandoc finds them through a second `--resource-path` entry (the locale's source folder).
- **Style:** `.screenshot` (75 % wide, framed, never split) and a plain italic `blockquote` for the example exchange.
- **Kindle check** (owner, 2026-10-01, the book built from the sample catalogue): downloaded and opened; the links
  open the browser; the screenshots are readable.
- **Follow-ups:** the owner's tone review is what is left.
