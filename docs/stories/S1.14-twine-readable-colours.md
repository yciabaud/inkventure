## User story

As a player of a Twine story on e-ink, I want every word and every link in black on white, so that I can read
dialogue and choices that the author coloured for a dark screen.

## Background

Found on 2026-10-01 (featured games, production, Kindle viewport). The e-ink stylesheet forces black on white on
`body` and on the passage containers, but not on the elements the author colours inside them. S1.11 already listed
this as a follow-up ("black text inside the story, not only on its containers").

- **Detritus** (`l8tr8m9tlkgix1sk`, SugarCube): the whole intro is light cyan (made for a dark starfield) on our
  white background.
- **The Den** (`392ffjwtupz0jsv8`, SugarCube): characters' lines ("Father!?", "Vee?") are pale beige and almost
  invisible.
- **A Long Way to the Nearest Star** (`6cjbax7d0to42vof`): links are forced to black, but their button background
  stays dark navy, so **every choice** is black on dark. The Settings and Restart icons at the top right are black
  on black.

On a 16-level grey screen, the author's colours carry little meaning anyway. Readability comes first.

## Scope

- Text: `color:#000!important` on every descendant of the story's containers (`tw-story`, `tw-passage`,
  `#passages`, `.passage`, `#story`, `main`, SugarCube's UI bar and dialogs), not only on the containers.
  Bold, italics and underlines stay.
- Backgrounds: `background-color:transparent!important; background-image:none!important` on links, buttons and
  inline elements inside passages. Keep `img`, `svg`, `canvas`, `video` and elements with a picture as their
  background (a title picture). Find a rule that tells them apart, and record it.
- Borders that carry a meaning (a button's outline) stay visible: black.
- Icon fonts and SVG icons in UI bars: visible in black on white.
- Check on all 5 featured Twine stories and the fixtures, and record anything still unreadable.
- SPEC §4.3 updated.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Detritus, The Den and A Long Way to the Nearest Star: every passage word and every link is black on white
      (or on a picture the author put there), on a desktop browser and on the Kindle. _Desktop: checked (Chromium,
      Kindle viewport, 8 screens each, a script finding no text that is not black on white). Kindle: to check._
- [x] Will Not Let Me Go and Birdland keep their pictures.
- [x] Links still look like links (bold, underlined, ≥ 48 px tall).

## Required tests

**Unit (Vitest)**

- The injected stylesheet forces descendant colours and clears link and button backgrounds.

**E2E (Playwright)**

- A Twine fixture (SugarCube) with coloured dialogue (`@@color:#f5deb3;…@@`), a link styled as a dark button and a
  title `<img>`. The text and the link's computed colours are black, the link background is transparent and the
  picture is still shown (read through the frame script or a test hook).

Plus the global gates: lint, `es-check es5` on the legacy bundle, size budgets, CI green.

## Out of scope

- An "original colours" option per story.
- Pictures made for a dark background (Detritus's title looks washed out but readable).

## Notes & decisions

- **Text:** `color` and `-webkit-text-fill-color` black on every element in `<body>` (and in a Harlowe story next to
  it), and on their `::before` / `::after` (icon fonts: SugarCube's UI bar, A Long Way's settings and restart
  icons). `-webkit-text-fill-color` also catches text drawn with a gradient (A Long Way's `.badend`).
- **Winning over the story's `!important` rules:** both are author `!important` rules, so specificity decides. Every
  new rule ends with `:not(#ik-0):not(#ik-1)` (two ids), which beats e.g. `.board button:hover{background-color:…
  !important}` (A Long Way). An inline `style="…!important"` still wins; none of the five stories has one on colours.
- **Backgrounds and pictures — the rule:** a background *colour* is never a picture, so it is cleared on every
  element. A background *image* is cleared on links and controls (`a`, `tw-link`, `button`, `input`…), where it is a
  button texture, and kept elsewhere: a `url(…)` is a picture, on a block or even on a `span` (the zip fixture's
  `.lamp-styled`, so the scope's "inline elements" became "links and controls"). `img`, `svg`, `canvas` and `video`
  are untouched (still grayscale). A gradient cannot be told from a picture in CSS: the frame script clears it when
  the element holds text (A Long Way's `.badend`), and keeps it otherwise (a gradient with no text is an icon or a
  decoration: Will Not Let Me Go's colour switch).
- **Borders:** links and buttons get black borders in CSS. Other elements: the frame script turns each visible border
  side black (style not `none`, width > 0, colour not transparent), so A Long Way's dialogue boxes (`3px ridge
  #b6e1fc`) are outlined in black and a transparent border kept for spacing (SugarCube's dialog lists, its loading
  spinner) stays invisible.
- **Kept opaque white:** SugarCube's `#ui-bar`, `#ui-dialog`, `#ui-overlay` and Harlowe's `tw-dialog` (with a black
  border) and `tw-backdrop`, so a dialog does not show the passage through it.
- **Disabled controls** are dark grey (`#555`), so they still look disabled.
- **SVG icons** in UI bars, links and buttons: `fill` / `stroke` attributes (other than `none`) turn black. None of
  the five featured stories uses SVG.
- **Symbols the device cannot draw** (found on the Kindle): Detritus's intro link is "⛛" (U+26DB), which no Kindle
  font has, so it showed an empty box. The frame script draws each symbol of a short link (≤ 8 characters) on a
  canvas, in the link's font, once that font is loaded (`document.fonts.check` / `load`: SugarCube's icon font is
  loaded only when its hidden dialog is first used). A symbol is missing when it draws nothing (Chromium) or the same
  pixels as characters no font has, U+FFFF, U+0378, U+10FFFD (a box). A link whose symbols are all missing shows the
  passage it leads to (SugarCube's `data-passage`, Harlowe's `passage-name`: "Start" here) or "Link" / "Lien"
  (`twine.symbolLink`); the symbol is kept in `data-ik-label`. Checked by running Chromium with only the DejaVu fonts
  (no U+26DB): the link reads "Start"; with the usual fonts, no link of the five stories is relabelled. A fallback
  symbol font was not an option: U+26DB is not even in DejaVu Sans, and covering the symbol blocks would cost ~45 KB
  per story.
- **Symbol fonts probe** (`probe/glyphs.html`, next to the S0.3 device probe): which fonts of the browser draw
  U+26DB and other symbols found in links, with the same canvas test, and whether each font name (generic families,
  the Kindle's reading fonts, usual symbol and emoji fonts) is installed. If the Kindle has a font with U+26DB that its
  browser does not use as a fallback, it can be added at the end of the e-ink stylesheet's font lists. **Kindle
  result (2026-10-02): no font draws U+26DB**, so the label stays the fix. The same page confirms the canvas test on
  the device: of the 14 symbols, it reports exactly the two shown as boxes as missing, U+26DB and U+2699 (⚙); ▼ ▶ ◀ →
  ← ✓ ✗ ★ ☰ ♥ © ✕ are drawn.
- **Detritus's logo** (`title_small.webp`, light glowing letters on transparency, made for the dark starfield) stays
  pale on white, as the scope expected. Telling such pictures apart by their pixels does not work: Birdland's portrait
  of Bridget (light hair on transparency) is lighter on average than the logo, so a rule inverting light pictures
  would invert her too.
- **Checked** (Chromium, Kindle viewport, production build, the real story files): Detritus, The Den and A Long Way to
  the Nearest Star, 8 screens each, with a script listing any element with text that is not black on white: none.
  Will Not Let Me Go and Birdland keep their pictures (illustration, character portraits).
- **Still lost:** what colour alone said. A selection shown only by colour (Detritus's difficulty buttons; SugarCube's
  settings toggles lose their green background, but their switch icon still shows on or off) and a bar drawn only
  with a background colour (none seen in the five stories) are no longer visible. Text hidden with
  `color:transparent` now shows (A Long Way defines `.transparent` but no passage uses it). Follow-up if a story needs
  it: an "original colours" option (out of scope).
