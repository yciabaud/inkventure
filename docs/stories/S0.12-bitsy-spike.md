## User story

As the product owner, I want to know whether a Bitsy game can be played on the Kindle, and how many Bitsy games we
could offer, so that Bitsy games can be decided on facts, before any catalogue or engine work.

## Background

[post-v1-ideas.md](../post-v1-ideas.md#bitsy) ranks **Bitsy** (Adam Le Doux's tiny tile-based game maker, MIT) as
promising: an avatar walks around 16 × 16-tile rooms and bumps into sprites to get dialogue. Read from the code
(Codeberg `adamledoux/bitsy`, v8.14, 2026-10-10):

- The engine (`editor/script/engine/`: `bitsy.js`, `world.js`, `script.js`, `dialog.js`, `renderer.js`, `font.js`,
  `transition.js`, `sound.js`) is **ES5**, about 57 KB gz with the system layer.
- It talks to the browser only through a small **system layer** (`editor/script/system/`: `system.js`,
  `graphics.js`, `input.js`, `soundchip.js`, ~32 KB raw). That layer is where the Kindle blockers are:
  `setInterval(updateSystem, 16)` (60 Hz), `new AudioContext()` at top level with no guard, keys and swipes only
  (no on-screen pad).
- A game is plain text (`<script type="text/bitsyGameData">` in an export). Palettes have three colours by default;
  tiles and sprites animate on two frames every 400 ms; dialogue has text effects (`{wvy}`, `{shk}`, `{rbw}`) and is
  shown character by character.
- The idea notes suggested our own player reading the data. Replacing only the **system layer** keeps the engine (its
  dialogue script language, its room logic) unchanged, so it is tried first.
- Almost no Bitsy game is on IFDB; ~6–12k are on itch.io *(unverified)*, and Ragzouken's
  [bitsy-archive](https://github.com/Ragzouken/bitsy-archive) gathered ~450 games **with each author's permission**.

## Scope

- A standalone page `public/probe/bitsy/` (no build dependency, like the Decker probe), with the pinned Bitsy engine
  (record version and licence, fetched and SHA-256-checked like Decker and Quixe) and **two real games** whose licence
  allows it (record each game, its author and its licence): a dialogue-heavy one and one that moves between several
  rooms. If none can be found in time, the editor's default game plus one written by us.
- **An e-ink system layer** in place of upstream `system/`, kept in the repo, with the engine files unchanged:
  - a frame is drawn only when the room, the avatar, a dialogue page or the palette changed; never on an idle frame;
    the loop stops when nothing is pending and an input starts it again;
  - two-frame animations frozen on frame 1; text effects drawn still; dialogue shown a page at a time (no
    character-by-character); transitions jump to their end;
  - palettes mapped to grays (record the mapping and how a three-colour room reads on the Kindle);
  - **an on-screen direction pad** with 48 px targets beside or under the room, and a tap on the room or the dialogue
    box to advance dialogue; keys still work;
  - no sound: `soundchip` stubbed, no `AudioContext`.
- **Measure on the Kindle**, shown on the page and as QR codes (like the probes): parse and start time, first room
  drawn, one step (tap on the pad to the avatar drawn), a room change, a dialogue page; CPU idle on a still room (no
  draw in 10 s); the room's zoom (128 × 128 scaled to the width, without smoothing) and whether the tiles read.
- **Size:** engine + e-ink system layer gz against the 100 KiB lazy-chunk budget (`size-budget.json`).
- **Catalogue reconnaissance**, recorded in the report, not built: how many games of bitsy-archive (and its licence
  terms) play with the stock engine of the pinned version, from their data only (no bitsy-hacks, no forks such as
  Bitsy HD or Bitsy Color): a script that loads each game's data headless with the engine and counts the ones that
  parse, and those using hacks or forks.
- A report in `docs/device-reports/` and a recommendation in `post-v1-ideas.md` (go: a story for a Bitsy engine and a
  curated shelf; or no-go, and why). SPEC §13 updated with the answer.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The probe page plays both games in Chromium with the e-ink system layer: the pad moves the avatar, a sprite
      starts its dialogue, a tap advances it, and no frame is drawn while nothing changes.
- [ ] A Kindle report recorded in `docs/device-reports/` with the measures above.
- [ ] The catalogue count (games that play from their data, with hacks or forks, licence terms) recorded.
- [ ] A go / no-go recommendation in `post-v1-ideas.md`, and SPEC §13 updated.

## Required tests

**Unit (Vitest)**

- The fetch script checks the pinned engine files' SHA-256 and fails loudly if they changed.
- The e-ink system layer: a draw is requested only after a change (a fake engine update that changes nothing draws
  nothing); a palette maps to grays.

**E2E (Playwright)**

- The probe page loads a game, a tap on the pad moves the avatar (the drawn frame changes), a tap advances a dialogue,
  and no draw happens on an idle room over a few seconds (counted by the system layer).

Plus the global gates: lint, size budgets (the probe is outside the app's budgets but its size is reported), CI green.
Bitsy is ES5, so `check:es5` covers `dist/probe/bitsy/` with no exception.

## Out of scope

- A Bitsy engine in the reader, catalogue entries, an itch.io scraper or permission requests (after the go / no-go).
- Sound, bitsy-hacks and forked engines (only counted).
- DAAD ([S0.13](S0.13-daad-spike.md)) and the other formats of `post-v1-ideas.md`.
