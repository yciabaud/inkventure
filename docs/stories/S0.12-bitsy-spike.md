## User story

As the product owner, I want to know whether a Bitsy game can be played on the Kindle, and how many Bitsy games we
could offer, so that Bitsy games can be decided on facts, before any catalogue or engine work.

## Background

[post-v1-ideas.md](../post-v1-ideas.md#bitsy) ranks **Bitsy** (Adam Le Doux's tiny tile-based game maker, MIT) as
promising: an avatar walks around 16 × 16-tile rooms and bumps into sprites to get dialogue. Read from the code
(Codeberg `adamledoux/bitsy`, v8.14, 2026-10-10):

- The engine (`editor/script/engine/`: `bitsy.js`, `world.js`, `script.js`, `dialog.js`, `renderer.js`, `font.js`,
  `transition.js`, `sound.js`) is **ES5**, about 57 KB gz with the system layer.
- It talks to the browser only through a small **system layer** (`editor/script/system/`: `system.js`,
  `graphics.js`, `input.js`, `soundchip.js`, ~32 KB raw). That layer is where the Kindle blockers are:
  `setInterval(updateSystem, 16)` (60 Hz), `new AudioContext()` at top level with no guard, keys and swipes only
  (no on-screen pad).
- A game is plain text (`<script type="text/bitsyGameData">` in an export). Palettes have three colours by default;
  tiles and sprites animate on two frames every 400 ms; dialogue has text effects (`{wvy}`, `{shk}`, `{rbw}`) and is
  shown character by character.
- The idea notes suggested our own player reading the data. Replacing only the **system layer** keeps the engine (its
  dialogue script language, its room logic) unchanged, so it is tried first.
- Almost no Bitsy game is on IFDB; ~6–12k are on itch.io *(unverified)*, and Ragzouken's
  [bitsy-archive](https://github.com/Ragzouken/bitsy-archive) gathered ~450 games **with each author's permission**.

## Scope

- A standalone page `public/probe/bitsy/` (no build dependency, like the Decker probe), with the pinned Bitsy engine
  (record version and licence, fetched and SHA-256-checked like Decker and Quixe) and **two real games** whose licence
  allows it (record each game, its author and its licence): a dialogue-heavy one and one that moves between several
  rooms. If none can be found in time, the editor's default game plus one written by us.
- **An e-ink system layer** in place of upstream `system/`, kept in the repo, with the engine files unchanged:
  - a frame is drawn only when the room, the avatar, a dialogue page or the palette changed; never on an idle frame;
    the loop stops when nothing is pending and an input starts it again;
  - two-frame animations frozen on frame 1; text effects drawn still; dialogue shown a page at a time (no
    character-by-character); transitions jump to their end;
  - palettes mapped to grays (record the mapping and how a three-colour room reads on the Kindle);
  - **an on-screen direction pad** with 48 px targets beside or under the room, and a tap on the room or the dialogue
    box to advance dialogue; keys still work;
  - no sound: `soundchip` stubbed, no `AudioContext`.
- **Measure on the Kindle**, shown on the page and as QR codes (like the probes): parse and start time, first room
  drawn, one step (tap on the pad to the avatar drawn), a room change, a dialogue page; CPU idle on a still room (no
  draw in 10 s); the room's zoom (128 × 128 scaled to the width, without smoothing) and whether the tiles read.
- **Size:** engine + e-ink system layer gz against the 100 KiB lazy-chunk budget (`size-budget.json`).
- **Catalogue reconnaissance**, recorded in the report, not built: how many games of bitsy-archive (and its licence
  terms) play with the stock engine of the pinned version, from their data only (no bitsy-hacks, no forks such as
  Bitsy HD or Bitsy Color): a script that loads each game's data headless with the engine and counts the ones that
  parse, and those using hacks or forks.
- A report in `docs/device-reports/` and a recommendation in `post-v1-ideas.md` (go: a story for a Bitsy engine and a
  curated shelf; or no-go, and why). SPEC §13 updated with the answer.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] The probe page plays both games in Chromium with the e-ink system layer: the pad moves the avatar, a sprite
      starts its dialogue, a tap advances it, and no frame is drawn while nothing changes.
- [x] A Kindle report recorded in `docs/device-reports/` with the measures above.
- [x] The catalogue count (games that play from their data, with hacks or forks, licence terms) recorded.
- [x] A go / no-go recommendation in `post-v1-ideas.md`, and SPEC §13 updated.

## Required tests

**Unit (Vitest)**

- The fetch script checks the pinned engine files' SHA-256 and fails loudly if they changed.
- The e-ink system layer: a draw is requested only after a change (a fake engine update that changes nothing draws
  nothing); a palette maps to grays.

**E2E (Playwright)**

- The probe page loads a game, a tap on the pad moves the avatar (the drawn frame changes), a tap advances a dialogue,
  and no draw happens on an idle room over a few seconds (counted by the system layer).

Plus the global gates: lint, size budgets (the probe is outside the app's budgets but its size is reported), CI green.
Bitsy is ES5, so `check:es5` covers `dist/probe/bitsy/` with no exception.

## Out of scope

- A Bitsy engine in the reader, catalogue entries, an itch.io scraper or permission requests (after the go / no-go).
- Sound, bitsy-hacks and forked engines (only counted).
- DAAD ([S0.13](S0.13-daad-spike.md)) and the other formats of `post-v1-ideas.md`.

## Notes & decisions

- **Pinned engine:** Bitsy **8.14**, Codeberg commit `2f5eecf8` (no tag), MIT, © Adam Le Doux and the Bitsy authors.
  `npm install` downloads the engine (`editor/script/engine/*.js`), upstream `system.js` and `graphics.js`, the
  default font (`ascii_small.bitsyfont`) and the editor's default game into `vendor/bitsy/` and checks their SHA-256
  (`scripts/build/bitsy-files.ts`, `fetch-bitsy.ts`), as for Decker and Quixe. The licence is committed there and
  shipped next to the runtime (`/probe/bitsy/LICENSE-bitsy.txt`).
- **Games:** no real game could be used. bitsy-archive's permission covers that archive and Bitsy Boutique only, not
  others, and itch.io games need their author's permission. So, as the scope allows: the editor's **default game**
  (one room, a cat, two items, dialogue with `{wvy}`; MIT with the engine) and **The Keeper's Lamp**, written for the
  probe (`public/probe/bitsy/keepers-lamp.bitsy`, this repository's licence): three rooms with a palette each (one
  red and yellow, to test the grays), an exit with a `slide_l` transition, an animated water tile, a gull with a
  dialogue of several pages (`{wvy}`), a key item, a keeper whose dialogue depends on the key, and an ending with
  `{rbw}`.
- **Engine unchanged.** The e-ink layer replaces only what the system layer exposes (`scripts/build/bitsy-eink/`, ES5):
  - `input.js` in place of upstream `input.js`: keys, a tap on the game, and the page's pad. A press is latched until
    a tick has read it, since on a slow device a tap can start and end between two ticks. No swipes, no restart
    combo;
  - `sound.js` in place of `soundchip.js`: no `AudioContext`, nothing plays;
  - `eink.js`, after the engine: the loop and the engine's settings. Upstream `system.js` already draws only the
    memory blocks that changed, so a tick that changes nothing draws nothing; what costs CPU is upstream's
    `setInterval(updateSystem, 16)`, forever. Here a tick runs on an animation frame only while an input is held or
    waits, or a dialogue page is being revealed, and the loop stops after two quiet ticks; the time step is capped
    at 100 ms. A dialogue page shows at once through the engine's own skip. The rest are engine globals:
    `animationTime = Infinity` (tiles and sprites keep their first frame), `transition = null` (exits jump to their
    room), `soundPlayer = null`; text effects are drawn still by holding the dialogue renderer's clock at 0 (their
    table is private to `dialog.js`).
- **Grays:** by default each room's colours are stretched by luminance from black to white (text box unchanged);
  `?gray=lum` (plain luminance) and `?gray=off` (colours) to compare. The report gives each room's smallest gray step.
- **Size:** the room is drawn at 512 × 512 (Bitsy's 128 px at ×4), on a canvas sized to the width and the height left
  by the pad, in steps of 128 px so that pixels stay whole without smoothing: 512 px on the Kindle's 636 × 740.
- **Page:** `public/probe/bitsy/` (static `index.html` + ES5 `probe.js`) downloads the game, the font and `runtime.js`
  (built by `scripts/build/bitsy-probe.ts`, a Vite plugin) with XHR, then runs the runtime from a `<script>` element.
  The pad is a cross of 64 × 56 px buttons (▲ ◀ ● ▶ ▼; ● reads on) under the room; `?game=default` switches game.
- **Size (`check:size` reports it, not budgeted):** `runtime.js` 243 KiB raw, **59 KiB gz** as served (unminified);
  minified with terser **29 KiB gz**, under a third of the 100 KiB lazy-chunk budget. All ES5: `check:es5` covers
  `dist/probe/bitsy/` with no exception.
- **Tests:** the runtime runs headless in jsdom (`scripts/build/bitsy-headless.ts`, canvases counted, frames run by
  hand) for the unit tests: a tick with no change draws nothing; a press moves the avatar one tile and is read even
  when released before the tick; a dialogue page is whole; an exit with a transition jumps to the next room.
- **Chromium baseline** (headless, 636 × 740, this machine): runtime parse 3 ms, start 2 ms, first room 15 ms after
  the start (the game parsed in the first tick, 10 ms); each input: first frame 14–15 ms (one animation frame),
  2 ticks, idle after ~31 ms; idle 10 s: 0 ticks, 0 draws.
- **bitsy-archive survey** (`scripts/survey/bitsy-archive.ts`, run on 2026-10-10 on the archive's last commit,
  2020-02-24): each game started headless with the pinned engine, tapped and walked four steps.

  | Games in the archive | 451 |
  |---|---|
  | Start, draw and take input with no error | 449 (100 %) |
  | … and no HD tiles nor hack tags | 407 (90 %) |
  | HD (16 × 16) tiles | 1 (0 %) |
  | Dialogue tags of bitsy-hacks | 41 (9 %) |
  | More than 3 colours in a palette | 43 (10 %) |
  | Text effects | 175 (39 %) |
  | Exit transitions | 0 (0 %) |
  | Animated tiles or sprites | 410 (91 %) |
  | One room | 65 (14 %) |
  | Bitsy versions (major) | 2: 11, 3: 99, 4: 285, 5: 39, none: 17 |

  Two games throw while starting (`reading 'src'`, `reading 'length'`; not investigated). "Plays" means no error, not a
  check of each game's logic: the archive's games are old (Bitsy 2–5, 2016–2020) and rely on the engine's backward
  compatibility. Almost all animate (91 %): on e-ink they would stand still on their first frame. **Licence:** the
  archive's permission is for that archive and Bitsy Boutique; a shelf of ours needs each author's own permission
  (the archive's `index.txt` lists 1,648 games with their authors and itch.io pages; `authors.txt` lists 96
  authors and what each agreed to).
- **Kindle run** (2026-10-10, PR #93 preview, [report](../device-reports/2026-10-10-kindle-bitsy.txt)), The
  Keeper's Lamp played to the lighthouse:
  - **Start:** downloads ~0.6 s (game, font, runtime), parse 21 ms, start 23 ms, first room **164 ms** later (the
    first tick parses the game, 143 ms): under a second from opening the page to a room.
  - **An input** (a step, a dialogue page): first frame **10–225 ms** after the tap, 2–3 ticks, the slowest tick
    9–31 ms and draw 8–25 ms; idle 0.3–0.6 s after the tap. That is the code's time: the e-ink repaint comes on top.
    Decker took 1.1–1.4 s for a card change on the same Kindle.
  - **Idle: 0 ticks, 0 draws in 10 s.**
  - **Screen** (the owner's photos): the room is sharp at 512 px (zoom 1) and the stretched grays read well. Two
    things to fix in an engine: `{wvy}` text drawn still keeps its letters offset up and down, which looks like a
    glitch (draw effect text flat instead); and partial-refresh **ghosting** leaves traces of the avatar's steps and
    of a closed dialogue box, worst on mid-gray floors (a full black/white refresh on a room change, as the app's
    *Refresh screen* does, and backgrounds pushed towards white or black).
  - **Input:** the pad works by touch (78 touches, `ontouchstart` absent as on S0.3); no `keydown`.
- **Recommendation: go** (written in [post-v1-ideas.md](../post-v1-ideas.md#bitsy), SPEC §13): Bitsy plays on the
  Kindle at a small fraction of Decker's cost, with the engine unchanged, and its runtime fits the lazy-chunk budget
  three times over. A Bitsy engine story would wrap the same e-ink layer in the reader, draw effect text flat, deal
  with ghosting (refresh on room change, black/white backgrounds), and feed a curated shelf of games with each
  author's permission (bitsy-archive's authors list is a start). The decision is the owner's.
