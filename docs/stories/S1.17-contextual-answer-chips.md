## User story

As a player answering a question the game asks in its text, I want to tap the answer, so that I don't type "yes"
or "3" on an e-reader's keyboard.

## Background

Found on 2026-10-01 (featured games, production). When a game asks a question and waits for a **line**, the command
bar shows the usual directions and verbs (N, S, E, W, Up, Down, Look…), which don't answer it:

- yes / no: Photopia ("Would you like instructions?"), Eat Me and The Bat ("Have you played interactive fiction
  before?"), Bronze (same question, then "Please answer yes or no.").
- Numbered conversation menus: The Impossible Bottle opens on four options "1. …" to "4. …" and keeps using them.
  Typing "1" works; nothing on screen says to.

## Scope

- When the last paragraph before the prompt asks a yes / no question (ends with `?` and the game language's words
  are known: EN "yes or no", "(y/n)", a question to the player; FR "oui ou non"), the **directions row** shows Yes
  and No chips (game language, like the other chips) that send the word. The verbs row and the field stay.
- When the paragraphs since the last input end with a numbered list (`1.` / `1)` / `(1)` …, at least 2 items, in
  order), the directions row shows the numbers as chips (`1` … `n`, at most 9). Each chip shows the number and,
  if room allows, the start of its option. A tap sends the number.
- The chips go away at the next turn, when the game asks something else.
- Be careful with false positives: a question in a room description must not replace the directions. Choose the
  rule (last paragraph only, before the prompt, …), give examples and record it.
- SPEC §3.6 (Command input) updated.

## Notes & decisions

- **The real texts** (the seven games run through ZVM / Quixe with their story files, the paragraphs before each line
  prompt):
  - Photopia: `Would you like instructions? ` with no `>` prompt at all. Eat Me and The Bat: `Have you played
    interactive fiction before?` then `>`. Bronze: `Have you played interactive fiction before? >`, the prompt in the
    same paragraph as the question.
  - The Impossible Bottle: `1. “But cleaning up is so boring!”` … `4. …` then `> `; after each answer a new list,
    renumbered from 1.
  - Counterfeit Monkey **does** open on questions: `Can you hear me? >> `, then `Do you remember our name?`. Both are
    yes/no questions the game accepts "yes" to, so Yes / No show there; its ordinary turns that follow (LOOK,
    INVENTORY, X ME) show the directions. Lost Pig: no chip on any turn tried (the intro, LOOK, X PIG, INVENTORY, N,
    LISTEN).
- **Yes/no rule** (`asksYesNo`, `src/reader/commands/answers.ts`), on the **last** non-blank paragraph since the last
  command, the prompt (`>`, `>>`) stripped from its end:
  - it says what it expects: "yes or no", "yes/no", "(y/n)", "[o/n]", "oui ou non" (Bronze's "Please answer yes or
    no.", with no question mark); or
  - it ends with "?" and its last sentence (quotes stripped) is a question **to the player**: an auxiliary verb first
    (do, would, will, can, shall, are, is, have, may, want…) and "you", "your", "I" or "we" in it, or a French
    inversion (`Voulez-vous`, `As-tu`, `Puis-je`) or `Est-ce que … vous/tu`.
  - Not taken: "Is that a light in the distance?" (no player), "What do you want to do?" / "Où voulez-vous aller ?"
    (not yes/no), a question followed by more text in the same paragraph, a question in an earlier paragraph.
- **Numbered rule** (`numberedOptions`): the paragraphs since the last command **end** with options on lines of their
  own, `1.` / `1)` / `(1)`, numbered from 1 in order, at least two; one short line (≤ 80 characters, ending with "?"
  or ":") may follow them. A gap, a list starting at 2, a single item, a list followed by more prose: no chips. Past 9
  options, the first 9 get chips (the others can be typed). A list wins over a yes/no question.
- **Where they go:** first in the directions row, in bold (`chip--answer`), the group named "Answers". The directions
  that no longer fit go to the ⋯ dialog as on narrow screens, so a wrong guess costs one tap more for a direction,
  never a lost direction. Hidden answers (a long list on a phone) are first in that dialog.
- **Labels:** an option's chip shows "1 — But cleaning up is so" (the option's first words up to a punctuation mark,
  ≤ 24 characters, not ending on an article or a preposition, like S1.12's key labels) when all the answers fit on the
  row with their words, else numbers only; the full label is the chip's accessible name either way. The Impossible
  Bottle's four options (about 200 px each with their words) should show as numbers on a Kindle-wide row: to see on
  the device.
- **Languages:** the chips follow the game language (Yes/No, Oui/Non, Sí/No, Ja/Nein, Sì/No). The detection covers
  English and French phrasing; Spanish, German and Italian games get chips only from a numbered list or "(y/n)".
- **Fixture:** `lamp.inf` has `BOAT` ("Do you want to call the boat back?", the library's `YesOrNo`, which asks again
  with "Please answer yes or no.") and `SIGNAL` (three numbered options, read as a line), rebuilt with Inform 6.41.
- **Owner's check (2026-10-03):** the chips are in the game's language ("Yes" / "No" for Bronze, even with a French
  UI), and at the Kindle size Bronze's question is on page 2 of 2, with the command bar. They look close to the
  direction chips (bold only); a stronger style was proposed and not needed.
